Recruiting field guide

Shared language for sport-specific evaluation.

Open a sport to review common recruiting terms and the context that can help make evaluations more useful. These are starting points, not universal standards.

Baseball

Tools: Hitting, throwing, fielding, and running abilities considered in the context of position. Exit velocity: How fast the ball leaves the bat. Projection: An informed view of how skills and physical tools may develop over time.

Basketball

Position size: Height, length, and strength compared with the demands of a role. Shot profile: The locations and situations from which an athlete scores. Transition: Play immediately after a change of possession.

Football

Positional fit: How an athlete’s traits translate to a specific role and scheme. Verified testing: Athletic measurements recorded under a known process. Film evaluation: Review of decisions, technique, and repeatable play traits across game situations.

Soccer

First touch: Control of the ball when receiving it. Scanning: Checking the field before receiving or making a play. Work rate: The repeated effort an athlete contributes in attacking and defensive phases.

Softball

Two-way value: Contributions in more than one role, such as pitching and hitting. Release point: Where a pitcher releases the ball. Situational hitting: Adjusting an at-bat to the game situation and team objective.

Volleyball

Serve receive: First contact used to control an opponent’s serve. Transition: Moving from defense into an organized attack. Position versatility: Ability to perform effectively in more than one role.

Track & field

Personal best: An athlete’s best recorded performance in an event. Split: A recorded time for part of a race or relay. Conversion: A context-aware comparison across events, conditions, or competition levels—not a guarantee of future results.

Lacrosse

Ground ball: Securing a loose ball on the field. Clearing: Moving possession from the defensive end toward attack. Field vision: Recognizing space, matchups, and passing options as play develops.

Evaluation terms can vary by coach, level, and program. Consider the source, setting, and date for every reported metric.
